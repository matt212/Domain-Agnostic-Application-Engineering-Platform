## 2. Discovery Objective

Independently determine what information the business requires to operate effectively and achieve its business outcomes.

Reason beyond the explicit information contained in the supplied inputs.

Understand the business context, actor responsibilities, business activities, interactions and business processes. From this understanding, discover the information that must be available, obtained, generated, communicated, maintained, evaluated or consumed for the business to function.

Do not simply extract existing business-object names, reproduce their attributes, or convert business activities into information requirements.

Discover the actual information needs arising from the business itself.

## 3. Required Reasoning

### A. Understand business information needs

For each relevant actor and business responsibility, independently determine:

- What information the actor needs to perform the responsibility.
- What information the actor needs before initiating or executing an activity.
- What information the actor must obtain from other actors, business operations or external parties.
- What information must be generated or updated through business activities.
- What information is required to monitor progress, evaluate results and complete responsibilities.

Consider both information explicitly represented in the inputs and information that the business logically requires but which has not yet been identified.

### B. Discover information required by business activities

Examine the supplied business activities and reason about the information needed throughout their execution.

Determine:

- Information required to initiate an activity.
- Information required to perform its individual operations.
- Information required to evaluate conditions and make business decisions.
- Information required to coordinate interactions between actors.
- Information generated as an activity progresses or completes.
- Information required to handle alternative outcomes, exceptions and unresolved situations.
- Information required by subsequent activities.

Do not assume that an activity's listed inputs and outputs fully describe its information requirements.

### C. Discover information supporting business decisions

Independently identify the decisions that business actors or automated business operations must make.

For each relevant decision, determine what information is necessary to make that decision and why.

Consider operational decisions, business-rule evaluations, eligibility, availability, prioritisation, financial decisions, coordination and exception handling wherever relevant to the actual business.

Do not force predefined decision categories onto the business. Discover them from the supplied context and your own business reasoning.

### D. Discover information beyond existing business objects

The supplied business objects are reference inputs, not the boundaries of information discovery.

Determine whether the business needs information that:

- Is not represented by an existing business object.
- Is obtained from external parties, services or systems.
- Is derived by evaluating or combining existing information.
- Is generated through business activities.
- Is needed temporarily to make a decision or execute an operation.
- Is required for coordination, monitoring, reconciliation or business control.

Do not automatically create new business objects for every information requirement.

An information requirement describes what information is needed by the business. A business object is a business entity that may hold or represent that information. They are related concepts, but they are not interchangeable.

### E. Examine information dependencies and continuity

Reason about how information moves across business activities and actors.

Identify information that must be available from one activity to another, information that must be updated as circumstances change, and information that must remain available to support subsequent business operations.

Consider whether missing, outdated, inconsistent or unavailable information could prevent an activity, decision or business outcome.

### F. Challenge the initial understanding

Do not accept the supplied actors, relationships, activities or business objects as necessarily complete.

Use your understanding of the business to identify additional information needs that the supplied model does not adequately explain.

Where an information requirement exposes a possible missing business activity, responsibility, actor interaction or business object, capture the information requirement itself. Do not regenerate or modify those other discovery dimensions in this pass.

## 4. Information Requirement Definition

An information requirement must describe a meaningful business information need, not an individual database column, technical field, API parameter, screen input or generic data attribute.

Each requirement should identify:

- **Information requirement:** What information the business needs.
- **Description:** What the information represents and what it covers.
- **Business purpose:** Why the information is needed and what business need it fulfils.
- **Required by actors:** Which actors need or consume this information.
- **Related responsibilities:** Which responsibilities require it.
- **Related activities:** Which activities use, generate or depend on it.
- **Related business objects:** Which existing business objects represent or contain the information, where applicable.
- **Information usage:** How the information is used, such as obtained, consumed, generated, evaluated, communicated, maintained or updated.
- **Information source:** Where the information originates or is obtained, where identifiable.
- **Business decisions supported:** Decisions that depend on this information.
- **Required information details:** The substantive information content necessary to fulfil the requirement.

Create distinct information requirements where the business purpose, usage, consumers or decision needs are materially different. Avoid splitting one coherent information need into multiple superficial requirements.

## 5. Discovery Constraints

1. Treat all supplied inputs as reference material, not a closed list of information requirements.
2. Independently reason about the business and discover information requirements rather than merely extracting information from existing objects.
3. Do not restrict discovery to the supplied actors, responsibilities, objects, relationships or activities.
4. Do not invent information needs without a meaningful business rationale.
5. Do not assume every actor needs access to every information requirement.
6. Do not assume every business activity requires a separate information requirement.
7. Do not duplicate information requirements using slightly different names.
8. Do not mistake business outcomes, activities, decisions or business objects for information requirements.
9. Do not turn information requirements into database schemas, technical designs, APIs or application features.
10. Do not regenerate business context, actors, responsibilities, business objects, relationships or activities.
11. Do not introduce a predefined catalogue of information requirement categories. Discover the relevant information needs from the business context.
12. Preserve meaningful business terminology from the supplied inputs.
