# 1. MISSION AND REASONING DISCIPLINE

Produce a realistic, domain-appropriate, evidence-grounded actor
authorization model. Use Domain and Niche as substantive sources of
business knowledge alongside the supplied business discovery data.

Remain fully domain-agnostic. Do not hardcode industry-specific actors,
objects, workflows, permissions, states, or rules. Do not assume every
industry follows the same operating model.

Internally distinguish:
1. SUPPLIED FACT: explicitly represented in the input.
2. REASONABLE BUSINESS INFERENCE: not explicitly stated as permission,
   but justified by the combined context and domain knowledge.
3. UNRESOLVED POSSIBILITY: plausible but dependent on unsupported
   assumptions or unresolved business decisions.

Use supplied facts and reasonable inferences. Exclude unresolved
possibilities. Do not output these classifications or your reasoning.

Before assigning any permission, determine:
- What business outcome and responsibilities define the actor's role?
- What do the supplied objects represent, and how do they relate to
  the actor's activities, decisions, dependencies, and outcomes?
- Who originates, maintains, changes, approves, executes, or records
  the relevant business information or action?
- What access or authority is necessary for the actor's responsibility?
- Is the capability a reasonable inference or an unsupported assumption?
- Are multiple operating models plausible? If so, include only authority
  justified across the supported context; otherwise omit the uncertain
  permission.

Do not invent business objects, exact fields, lifecycle states, thresholds,
policies, approval hierarchies, or relationships without sufficient support.

# 2. DISCOVER ACTORS AND RESPONSIBILITIES

- Is access limited to specific information or records?

Visibility does not imply modification authority. Do not grant broad access
merely because an actor coordinates, administers, operates, or participates

# 4. WHAT THEY CAN CREATE

- SUBMIT: supply information to another participant or system.
- TRIGGER: initiate a process that may create an object.
- UPDATE: change an existing object.

Determine whether the actor creates the object directly or merely submits
information or triggers another participant or system. Do not infer creation
authority from visibility, participation, responsibility, submission,
confirmation, or process initiation alone.

Use exact business-object names from the supplied data. Never invent
objects for implied records, events, calculations, or process results.
Do not invent automated participants to explain unspecified behavior.

# 5. WHAT THEY CAN CHANGE — REQUIRED CONTEXTUAL INFERENCE

## 5.1 Actor-object investigation

For every materially relevant supplied business object, investigate:

1. Does the actor's responsibility require maintaining or correcting
   information on this existing object?
2. Does the actor supply information that becomes part of the object?
   Who directly records or modifies that information?
3. Does the actor's activity require recording an outcome against an
   existing object?
4. Could the supported work involve changing information, quantity,
   assignment, relationship, instructions, decision, or status?
5. Does the actor handle corrections, discrepancies, rework, or changing
   circumstances supported by the context?
6. Which exact supplied object and what specific business information
   would change, and for what purpose?
7. Does the actor perform the change directly or only request, propose,
   approve, or trigger it?
8. Does another participant or automated process perform the actual change?
9. What supported conditions or dependencies constrain the authority?
10. What combined contextual evidence and domain reasoning justify it?

## 5.2 Domain-informed inference

Treat the supplied discovery data as the context for understanding the
business, not as the exclusive source of explicitly stated permissions.

Ask independently for each actor:
- What existing information would this actor plausibly maintain or change
  to fulfil their business responsibilities in this Domain and Niche?
- Which supplied objects would they reasonably modify, given each object's
  business meaning and the actor's operational role?
- Which direct changes are reasonable domain-based inferences even when
  no explicit update activity or permission is supplied?
- Which changes depend on unresolved policies, approval rules, or
  operating-model decisions?

Do not require an explicit update statement or field-level description
before considering a modification. Include reasonable, narrowly scoped
permissions supported by the combined context and domain knowledge.

Do not invent exact fields, policies, states, or authority. Describe
changeable information in business language and use exact supplied
business-object names.

## 5.3 Distinguish change mechanisms

Keep these mechanisms separate:
- Directly modifying an existing object.
- Submitting information for another participant to record.
- Requesting or proposing a modification.
- Approving a proposed modification.
- Triggering a process that changes an object.
- Performing an activity whose outcome is automatically recorded by a system.

Do not assign system-executed modifications to a human without independent
support for that actor's direct modification authority. If an actor initiates
an action and the system applies the change, describe the supported action
under Operations_They_Can_Run; do not automatically grant direct change
authority.

Treat status transitions as changes to existing objects unless the supplied
data establishes a separate business object.

If a specific modification is supported, include it.

If the evidence does not establish direct modification authority, do not
invent it. An empty array is correct when no change can reasonably be
established after this investigation.

# 6. OPERATIONS THEY CAN RUN

Derive Operations_They_Can_Run independently for every actor.

Identify meaningful business actions, decisions, and process steps supported
by the actor's responsibilities and the context.

For each candidate determine:
- What action is performed, and what outcome does it achieve?
- Which responsibility and supplied objects support the operation?
- Does it create, modify, initiate, decide, or act without modifying?
- What supported prerequisites, dependencies, or conditions apply?
- Does the actor execute it directly or request another participant or
  system to execute it?

Distinguish viewing from acting; creating from modifying; modifying from
executing; requesting from approving; initiating from executing; and
human authorization from automated execution.

Do not infer cancellation, approval, reassignment, override, settlement,
dispute resolution, or other operations merely because they are common
in an industry. Do not output technical APIs, HTTP methods, database
commands, UI implementation details, or executable system commands as
business operations.

# 7. CROSS-DIMENSION CONSISTENCY AND FINAL REVIEW

Evaluate all four authorization dimensions independently, then verify:

- Visibility supports the actor's actual information needs.
- Creation authority reflects responsibility for originating the object.
- Direct modification authority is independently justified.
- Initiation, approval, execution, and automated consequences are assigned
  to the appropriate participants.
- Object names match the supplied data.
- Permissions are not copied across actors without justification.
- Scope is no broader than the supported responsibility.
- Each permission has a defensible business purpose.
- No unsupported assumptions or contradictions remain.

Check for missing actor responsibilities; overlooked but reasonably supported
changes; empty change arrays resulting from insufficient investigation;
invented changes added merely to populate arrays; excessive visibility;
confusion between creation, submission, and triggering; confusion between
direct and automated updates; unsupported actors, objects, attributes,
states, conditions, or operations; duplicate or vague permissions; and
industry conventions incorrectly treated as universal rules.

Do not infer one authorization dimension automatically from another.
Do not output reasoning, questions, classifications, or review notes.
