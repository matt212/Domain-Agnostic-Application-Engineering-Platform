============================================================
PRIMARY OBJECTIVE
=================

Discover the most complete REAL-WORLD BUSINESS MODEL that is
supported by the supplied evidence.

The objective is:

MAXIMUM EVIDENCE-SUPPORTED BUSINESS COVERAGE
+
ZERO UNSUPPORTED INVENTION

The business model must be specific to the discovered business
domain.

Do not generate a generic industry template.

Do not force the business into a predefined workflow.

Do not fill missing information using general industry knowledge.

There is NO minimum number of business objects.

There is NO minimum number of activities.

There is NO required lifecycle shape.

There is NO required actor structure.

There is NO required number of stages.

Include only what the evidence supports.

============================================================
DOMAIN DISCOVERY
================

First determine the actual operating domain from the supplied
evidence.

The domain must describe HOW THE BUSINESS OPERATES, not merely the
industry name.

For example:

Weak:
"Retail"

Better:
"Online grocery marketplace"

Weak:
"Healthcare"

Better:
"Outpatient healthcare service provider"

Weak:
"Banking"

Better:
"Retail banking and financial services"

Use the most specific operating model supported by evidence.

Do not invent specificity that the evidence does not support.

============================================================
PRIMARY REFERENCE BUSINESS
==========================

Identify the specific real-world business that most closely
represents the BUSINESS IDEA and supplied evidence.

Prefer a specific business/company/organization when evidence
supports one.

Do not select a reference business merely because it is famous.

The reference business is an evidence source, not a template.

Do not automatically copy every capability of the reference business.

Only include capabilities that are:

1. supported by supplied evidence, and
2. relevant to the BUSINESS IDEA.

============================================================
HARD EVIDENCE GATE
==================

Every discovered element must pass an evidence gate.

BUSINESS OBJECT:

Include only when the supplied evidence explicitly identifies
the entity or clearly demonstrates that the business materially
handles that entity.

BUSINESS ACTIVITY:

Include only when the supplied evidence explicitly describes
the business operation or clearly demonstrates that the operation
occurs.

ALTERNATIVE PATH:

Include only when the supplied evidence explicitly describes
a materially different business-process variation.

If evidence is insufficient:

OMIT THE ELEMENT.

Never invent an element merely because it is common in the domain.

Never use:

"Most businesses have this."

"This is normally required."

"This industry usually does this."

"This would probably happen."

as justification.

============================================================
BUSINESS OBJECT DISCOVERY
=========================

Discover the REAL-WORLD ENTITIES that materially participate in
the discovered business operation.

The object model must emerge from the evidence.

Do NOT use a predefined object list.

Do NOT assume objects from another industry.

A business object may represent any meaningful real-world entity
handled by the business, such as a:

These are examples of possible categories only.

Do not include an example unless the evidence supports it.

============================================================
OBJECT QUALITY
==============

Business objects must represent meaningful real-world entities.

Do NOT create objects for:

screens
pages
buttons
forms
APIs
databases
tables
software components
technical services
fields
attributes
validation rules
notifications
status values
simple decisions
UI controls

Do not split one real-world entity into artificial objects.

Do not create duplicate or synonymous objects.

If multiple terms represent the same real-world entity, consolidate
them into one object.

Use the terminology supported by the discovered domain.

============================================================
BUSINESS ACTIVITY DISCOVERY
===========================

Discover materially distinct REAL-WORLD BUSINESS OPERATIONS.

Activities must emerge from the evidence and the discovered
operating model.

Do NOT use a predefined activity list.

An activity represents a meaningful business operation performed
to create, modify, process, deliver, manage, resolve, or complete
a business outcome.

The activity model must be appropriate to the discovered domain.

============================================================
ACTIVITY MATERIALITY
====================

Do NOT create separate activities merely for:

validation
calculation
authorization
notification
confirmation
status changes
simple decisions
UI interaction
screen navigation
field entry
technical processing

When these occur as part of a genuine business operation, represent
them as steps or alternative handling when supported by evidence.

Create a separate activity only when the evidence demonstrates
a distinct business purpose and materially distinct lifecycle.

============================================================
ACTIVITY CONSOLIDATION
======================

Before creating an activity, determine whether it is actually:

1. a step inside another activity,
2. an exception within another activity,
3. a supporting operation,
4. a validation,
5. a calculation,
6. a notification,
7. a status transition,
8. or a genuinely independent business operation.

Only the final category should normally become a separate activity.

Prefer coherent business processes over artificial fragmentation.

============================================================
DOMAIN-SPECIFIC LIFECYCLE DISCOVERY
===================================

Do NOT impose a predefined lifecycle.

Instead discover the lifecycle from the evidence.

Determine, where applicable:

How does the business process begin?

What business object initiates it?

What activities occur?

What transformations or business operations occur?

What constitutes progress?

What constitutes completion?

What happens when the normal process cannot continue?

What exceptions or alternative paths exist?

What happens after completion?

The lifecycle may differ completely between domains.

For example, one domain may involve:

Request
→ Assessment
→ Approval
→ Fulfillment
→ Completion

Another may involve:

Reservation
→ Confirmation
→ Service Delivery
→ Completion

Another may involve:

Claim
→ Investigation
→ Assessment
→ Decision
→ Settlement

These are illustrative patterns only.

Do NOT copy them unless supported by evidence.

============================================================

For the discovered domain, identify all materially important

Only include categories supported by evidence.

The actual lifecycle may contain different concepts.

============================================================
ALTERNATIVE PATH DISCOVERY
==========================

Identify materially different business-process variations.

An alternative path must change how the business operation proceeds.

Examples of generic variation types include:

failure
rejection
unavailability
cancellation
substitution

manual handling
alternative fulfillment
alternative service delivery
exception resolution

These are categories only.

Discover the actual domain-specific alternatives from evidence.

Do NOT create alternatives for:

dropdown selections
field values
UI choices
ordinary navigation
minor validation errors
notifications
simple status changes

Do not create duplicate alternatives.

============================================================
BUSINESS OBJECT → ACTIVITY MAPPING
==================================

Every activity must reference the business objects it materially
handles.

Do not reference an object merely because it is indirectly related.

Every business object must be used by at least one activity.

Every activity must use at least one business object.

============================================================
ACTIVITY STEPS
==============

Each activity must contain 2–4 concise BUSINESS OPERATIONS.

Steps must describe real-world business work.

Do not describe:

screens
buttons
UI navigation
database operations
API calls
technical implementation
software behavior

Steps should normally contain 3–8 words.

============================================================
EVIDENCE CONFLICTS
==================

When supplied evidence conflicts:

Prefer stronger and more authoritative evidence.

Do not merge contradictory claims.

Do not invent a compromise capability.

============================================================
EVIDENCE GAPS
=============

Missing evidence is NOT permission to infer.

If an important capability is not supported:

OMIT IT.

Do not manufacture completeness.

A smaller evidence-supported model is preferable to a larger
invented model.

============================================================
REFERENCE BUSINESS BOUNDARY
===========================

Do not reproduce the entire business model of the reference
organization.

Discover only the capabilities relevant to:

BUSINESS IDEA:
online groceries store

and supported by the supplied evidence.

================

Before producing the JSON, verify:

1. The domain was discovered from evidence.
2. No industry-specific assumptions were imposed.
3. The reference business is evidence-supported.
4. The operating model is evidence-supported.
5. Every business object passes the evidence gate.

9. No artificial activities exist.
10. Every object is used by an activity.

18. Unsupported capabilities are excluded.
19. No capability was added because it is common in the industry.
20. Output is valid JSON.
