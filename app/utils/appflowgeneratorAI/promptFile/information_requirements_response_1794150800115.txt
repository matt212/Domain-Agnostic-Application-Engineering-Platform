## 3. ASSESSMENT OBJECTIVE

Assess EVERY Information Requirement in SOURCE 1.

Evaluate ONLY the following three dimensions.

### 3.1 BUSINESS DECISION SUFFICIENCY

Determine whether the information represented by the requirement is sufficient to support the relevant business decisions.

Assess:

- What actual business decisions require this information?
- Are the relevant decisions identifiable from the business context?
- Does the requirement provide sufficient information to make those decisions?
- Are the required decision conditions identifiable?
- Are decision outcomes understandable?
- Is critical decision-making information missing?
- Are the stated business decisions supported by the supplied business activities and actor responsibilities?
- Are there ambiguities that would prevent a business decision from being implemented reliably?

Do not invent business decisions.

Do not assume that every requirement must support multiple decisions.

Where the source evidence does not establish a decision, explicitly identify the evidence limitation.

### 3.2 FUNCTIONAL RULES AND INFORMATION VALIDATION SUFFICIENCY

Determine whether the requirement contains sufficient functional rules and information validation details for subsequent functional analysis.

Assess:

- Are the stated functional rules meaningful and understandable?
- Are the business conditions and constraints sufficiently defined?
- Are the rules supported by the supplied business discovery evidence?
- Are required validation conditions identifiable?
- Are expected validation outcomes understandable?
- Are invalid conditions and relevant exceptions addressed?
- Are conditional rules sufficiently clear?
- Are there contradictory rules or validation conditions?
- Are critical functional rules or validation requirements missing?
- Can a functional analyst derive implementable functional specifications without making unsupported assumptions?

Distinguish between:

1. Explicitly defined functional rules.
2. Rules supported by the supplied business evidence.
3. Rules that require business clarification.
4. Missing rules that materially affect functional correctness.

Do not invent functional rules.

Do not impose generic validation rules.

Do not demand technical implementation details or application-specific validation unless supported by the supplied evidence.

### 3.3 REQUIRED INFORMATION DETAILS COMPLETENESS

Determine whether the requirement identifies sufficient information details to fulfil its business purpose and support its associated decisions, functional rules and validation.

Assess:

- Are the required information attributes sufficiently specific?
- Are important business attributes missing?
- Are relevant statuses and classifications identified?
- Are required measurements, values, dates, identifiers or contextual details adequately represented where applicable?
- Are information details sufficiently meaningful to support business decisions?
- Are details sufficient to define relevant functional rules?
- Are details sufficient to define validation conditions?
- Are important dependencies between information attributes identified?
- Are information details ambiguous, redundant or unnecessarily broad?
- Would a functional analyst need to invent important information attributes to proceed?

Assess completeness according to the actual business context and the purpose of the individual requirement.

Do not impose a universal mandatory attribute list.

Do not assume every requirement needs dates, identifiers, statuses, measurements or classifications.

Only identify missing details when supported by actual business evidence or when their absence materially prevents the stated business purpose from being fulfilled.

## 4. EVIDENCE AND SOURCE INTERPRETATION

Use the supplied discovery artefacts as the primary evidence.

Interpret the sources together where necessary.

Use:

- Business Objects to understand the information represented.
- Actors to understand who needs or uses the information.
- Responsibilities and Authorizations to understand why the information is needed.
- Actor–Business Object Relationships to understand established business relationships.
- Business Activities to understand the operational context and information usage.
- Information Requirements to establish the actual information needs being assessed.

Distinguish:

- Explicitly established information.
- Semantically supported information.
- Reasonable but unverified interpretations.
- Missing evidence.
- Genuine deficiencies.

A missing exact textual reference does not automatically establish a deficiency.

Do not treat an unrecorded detail as mandatory without business evidence.

Do not replace actual business context with generic Quick Commerce assumptions.
