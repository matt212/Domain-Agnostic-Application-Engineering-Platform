ANALYSIS INSTRUCTIONS

1. Extract existing capabilities

For each actor, retrieve the exact existing array from:

access_authorization_and_scope.What_They_Can_Change

Preserve the original capability descriptions in the output. If the property is absent or is not an array, return an empty array for the extracted existing capabilities and record the input issue.

Do not substitute What_They_Can_See, What_They_Can_Create, or Operations_They_Can_Run for What_They_Can_Change.

2. Understand the business context

Use the supplied domain and niche to understand the business processes, information, entities, decisions, exceptions, and operational responsibilities relevant to each actor.

Use the available actor data to establish the actor's actual role, business interactions, existing authorization scope, and relationship to the business process.

Do not use or reference core_responsibilities.

3. Identify missing change capabilities

Evaluate what the actor can already change and determine whether additional business-relevant change capabilities are missing.

Consider the complete supplied context, including relevant business objects, lifecycle stages, process transitions, exception scenarios, and downstream business impacts when those are available in the input.

Ask:

- What existing business information may this actor legitimately need to amend or correct?
- What relevant business attributes, statuses, assignments, decisions, or records are currently changeable only partially or not represented?
- What changes are needed to handle realistic business exceptions and recovery scenarios?
- Are there distinct business changes that the current capabilities incorrectly combine into one broad capability?
- Are there relevant changes required to complete the actor's business interactions that are not covered by the existing list?

Generate domain- and niche-specific candidates from the supplied context and applicable business knowledge. Do not rely on hardcoded actor-to-capability mappings or a predefined capability list.

4. Validate every proposed addition

For each proposed capability:

- Ensure it is not already present, including under different wording.
- Ensure it represents a genuine change to business information or business state.
- Ensure it is relevant to the supplied domain, niche, and actor.
- Explain briefly why the capability is missing and why it is relevant.
- Distinguish a justified business need from an assumption about the actor's authority.

Do not propose capabilities merely because they are technically possible.

Do not assume that an actor should have permission to change every record they can see or create.

Do not automatically assign sensitive or privileged changes, such as financial adjustments, approval decisions, security settings, or final business-state overrides, to an actor without contextual justification.

If a change is a plausible business requirement but the supplied information does not establish that the actor should be authorized to perform it, identify it as requiring business validation rather than presenting it as an established permission.

5. Maintain capability boundaries

Keep change capabilities distinct from:

- Viewing or accessing information.
- Creating new records.
- Executing operations or commands.
- Approving or authorizing actions, unless the action explicitly changes a business record or state
