## 2. ANALYSIS A — MANDATORY ACTOR CAPABILITY DISCOVERY
============================================================

### 2.1 Discover what each existing actor must additionally be able to do

For every existing actor, examine its supplied content and independently
derive the additional capabilities necessary for it to fulfil its role.

Base this reasoning on:

- The actor's identity and role in the inferred operating model.
- Its declared responsibilities and expected outcomes.
- The business purpose and niche.
- The business entities and interactions relevant to its work.
- Its dependencies on other actors and systems.
- The actions, decisions, and outcomes necessary to fulfil its responsibilities.
- Additional requirements implied by the actual business operating model.

Do not restrict discovery to the actions, permissions, fields, capabilities,
or workflows explicitly mentioned in the generated content.

For every actor, ask:

"What else must this actor be able to do, beyond what is already listed,
for its role and responsibilities to function correctly?"

"Which necessary capabilities are implied by the actor's purpose, business
responsibilities, or expected outcomes but are absent from the supplied data?"

"What information or business entities must the actor be able to interact
with, create, change, update, maintain, or manage for its responsibilities
to be executable?"

"What necessary decisions, actions, permissions, and operational outcomes
are missing from this actor's definition?"

"Can the actor complete its required responsibilities using only the
capabilities represented or otherwise provided by the existing system?"

"Which missing capability would actually prevent the actor from fulfilling
a required responsibility or prevent the business from achieving its outcome?"

Derive the answers independently for each actor.

Do not use a predefined catalogue of actors, capabilities, features, actions,
business objects, or lifecycle stages as the definition of completeness.

The questions above guide reasoning; they do not imply that every actor
must support every type of operation.

### 2.2 Derive capabilities from responsibilities and business context

For each actor, reason from its purpose to the capabilities necessary to
execute its responsibilities end to end.

Determine:

- What outcomes the actor must achieve.
- What information and business entities it must access or act upon.
- What it must initiate, create, perform, change, maintain, or manage.
- What it must inspect, decide, authorize, execute, or complete.
- Which changes it must be permitted to make and which it must not make.
- What prerequisites and permissions are necessary for its actions.
- What it needs from other actors and systems.
- What must happen after it performs a required action.
- How the required outcome is established.
- What must happen when a required operation cannot be completed.
- What additional capabilities are necessary but absent from the supplied data.

These are reasoning questions, not a mandatory list of capabilities.

Derive only operations that are applicable to the actor's actual role and
business operating model.

Do not assume that every conceivable capability must be implemented.

### 2.3 Discover missing capabilities beyond existing data

The supplied content may describe only a subset of what an actor can do.

Independently derive additional capabilities from the actor's role,
responsibilities, expected outcomes, and the domain-and-niche operating model.

Do not require a missing capability to have an existing field, permission,
action name, business object, or workflow step in the supplied artifact.

However, the absence of a capability from the artifact does not by itself
establish that the capability is mandatory.

For every candidate capability, determine:

1. What established business responsibility or required outcome makes it
   necessary?
2. Why does the actor need the capability to fulfil that responsibility?
3. What is specifically missing from the supplied artifact?
4. Is the capability already provided by another actor, system, workflow,
   permission, or equivalent mechanism?
5. What would fail or become impossible without it?
6. What is the smallest correction needed to make the responsibility executable?

A capability is a confirmed gap only when it is necessary for the defined
operating model and is not adequately provided by the existing arrangement.

If the capability is merely useful, convenient, desirable, or potentially
beneficial, do not report it as a mandatory gap.

### 2.4 Examine necessary operational implications

For every mandatory capability candidate, examine whether the actor has the
necessary information, permissions, preconditions, validation, execution
ability, resulting state changes, downstream effects, and completion evidence.

Identify missing implications only when their absence would prevent a
required operation or make its outcome invalid or incomplete.

Do not automatically create a separate workflow, permission, business object,
or finding for every capability.

Prefer one coherent correction when it resolves a single underlying
deficiency. Keep genuinely independent mandatory gaps separate.

## 3. ANALYSIS B — MANDATORY CROSS-ACTOR OPERATIONAL GAP DISCOVERY
============================================================

Independently inspect how the business operates across actors and systems.

Do not limit this analysis to the responsibilities or capabilities of any
single actor.

The objective is to determine whether the complete operating model can
execute its required business workflows correctly.

### 3.1 Responsibility and ownership continuity

Determine whether every necessary business responsibility has an adequate
owner or executing mechanism.

Identify a gap when:

- A required activity has no accountable owner or execution mechanism.
- An actor is assigned an outcome but cannot achieve it with the available
  capabilities or authority.
- Responsibilities conflict in a way that prevents required operations.
- A necessary handoff leaves the receiving actor unable to proceed.
- A required decision cannot be made by any authorized participant.
- A required operation is assumed to be covered but is not actually provided.

Do not assume that a missing responsibility requires a new human actor.

### 3.2 Operational dependencies and handoffs

Trace how actors and systems depend on each other.

Determine whether:

- Required inputs and prerequisites exist before an operation begins.
- The output of one required activity enables the next activity.
- Ownership and responsibility transfer correctly.
- Required decisions and outcomes reach the relevant actors or systems.
- A downstream activity cannot begin before its mandatory prerequisites.
- Changes to one record or operation leave dependent records and activities
  in a valid, consistent condition.
- The required business outcome is reached and made available to the
  appropriate actor or system.

Report only dependencies and handoffs whose omission prevents or materially
invalidates a required business operation.

Do not report hypothetical improvements to coordination or communication.

### 3.3 Decisions, transitions, and lifecycle continuity

Examine the relevant business workflows from initiation through their
required outcomes.

Determine whether the supplied content represents the decisions, permitted
transitions, preconditions, postconditions, and outcomes necessary for the
actual operating model.

Identify mandatory gaps such as an essential transition that is impossible,
a required outcome that is unreachable, a contradictory condition that blocks
execution, or a state change that leaves the business operation invalid.

Do not impose a universal lifecycle or state model on every business object.

Derive lifecycle requirements from the actual business context.

### 3.4 Required failure handling and recovery

Independently reason about failures and exceptional conditions that can
prevent a required business operation from completing correctly.

Determine whether the operating model provides the necessary means to
preserve correctness, maintain valid business state, and reach a valid
completion or recovery outcome when such conditions occur.

Identify gaps only when the missing handling is necessary for the defined
operation to remain valid or recoverable.

Do not report every conceivable failure scenario as a separate requirement.

Do not invent retries, escalations, recovery roles, compensation operations,
or exception workflows without establishing their necessity.

### 3.5 Authorization and operational control

Determine whether actors have the permissions and authority necessary to
perform their required responsibilities.

Identify mandatory gaps where an operation cannot be performed because
authority or permission is missing, or where conflicting authority makes
the required operating model invalid.

Distinguish between requesting, approving, executing, and recording an
operation where those distinctions matter to the business.

Do not invent approval layers, separation-of-duties rules, organizational
hierarchies, or additional controls without establishing that they are
necessary in the supplied operating model.

## 5. STRICT MUST-HAVE VALIDATION
============================================================

Before reporting any candidate, establish all of the following:

A. NECESSITY

Identify the specific responsibility, required business outcome, or mandatory
operational dependency that makes the capability necessary.

B. ACTUAL DEFICIENCY

Identify precisely what is missing, inaccessible, incomplete, or contradictory
in the supplied artifact.

C. OPERATIONAL CONSEQUENCE

Explain what required action, responsibility, workflow, or business outcome
would fail, become impossible, or become invalid without the capability.

D. EXISTING-COVERAGE CHECK

Establish that an existing actor, system, workflow, permission, or equivalent
mechanism does not already provide the required behavior.

E. MINIMAL-CORRECTION CHECK

Determine the smallest correction that restores the required capability or
operational behavior without unnecessarily expanding the operating model.

If these conditions cannot be established, do not report the candidate as
a confirmed mandatory gap.

The following are insufficient reasons to report a gap:

- A capability is common in the industry.
- A feature could improve convenience, experience, or efficiency.
- An operation could be automated.
- An additional actor could provide specialization.
- An extra control could reduce a hypothetical risk.
- A process could be performed more frequently.
- A new reporting, monitoring, training, auditing, or optimization function
  might benefit the business.
- A capability is not explicitly listed in the supplied artifact.

Do not infer mandatory requirements from desirability alone.

The test is whether the defined business operating model requires the
capability to function correctly, not whether the capability would be useful.

## 6. DETERMINE THE SMALLEST VALID CORRECTION
============================================================

For every confirmed mandatory gap, identify the smallest correction that
restores the required operation.

Evaluate these alternatives:

1. Add a missing capability to an existing actor.
2. Correct or extend an existing responsibility.
3. Correct a missing or incorrect authorization.
4. Add or correct a required action, condition, or transition.
5. Correct a mandatory cross-actor handoff or dependency.
6. Add a necessary system capability or execution mechanism.
7. Introduce a new actor only when a distinct accountability, ownership,
   independence, or authorization boundary is necessary.
8. Request business clarification when a material decision is unresolved.

Do not automatically introduce a new actor because a capability is absent.

Before recommending a new actor, establish why existing actors or systems
cannot reasonably fulfil the required responsibility.

Do not automatically assign every missing capability to an existing actor
either. Preserve justified responsibility and authorization boundaries.

Do not invent organizational structures, business policies, fields,
permissions, actor identifiers, or JSON paths.

If a material business decision prevents a defensible correction, retain the
issue for clarification without inventing a patch.

## 7. FINAL FINDING SELECTION
============================================================

Perform a complete discovery before selecting findings.

- Examine every existing actor for missing mandatory capabilities.
- Examine all relevant cross-actor dependencies and operational outcomes.
- Continue the review after identifying initial gaps.
- Re-examine actors affected by discovered workflow deficiencies.
- Consolidate symptoms caused by the same underlying deficiency.
- Keep independent deficiencies separate when they require different fixes.
- Exclude capabilities already provided by an existing mechanism.
- Exclude good-to-have enhancements and speculative requirements.
- Exclude optional actors, processes, automation, and controls.
- Do not use a predefined capability catalogue or expected-actor list.
- Do not manufacture findings to reach a numerical target.

A potential capability may be discovered without becoming a reported finding.

A finding is permitted only when the capability is mandatory and the supplied
artifact has a material deficiency that prevents the required responsibility
or workflow from functioning correctly.

Zero findings is valid when no mandatory operational gaps are established.

decision in placement_instruction.

Use NO_CHANGE only for material uncertainty that prevents a defensible
correction. Do not use it to avoid correcting a confirmed gap.

Never invent an existing identifier, field, permission, actor, JSON path,
or artifact structure.

If the exact path cannot be verified, set target_path to null and identify
the closest verifiable location in placement_instruction.

Every finding must reference exactly one problem_id from the problems array.
Every problem must have exactly one corresponding finding.

Do not include undeclared fields in either array.
