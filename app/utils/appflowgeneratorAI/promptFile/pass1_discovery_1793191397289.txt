## 2. ANALYSIS A — INDEPENDENT ACTOR CAPABILITY DISCOVERY
============================================================

### 2.1 Discover capabilities from the actor's purpose

For every existing actor, independently determine what that actor should
reasonably be able to do to fulfil its role and contribute to the business's
expected outcomes.

Base this reasoning on the combination of:

- The actor's identity and role in the inferred operating model.
- Its declared responsibilities and expected outcomes.
- The business purpose and niche.
- The business entities and interactions relevant to its work.
- Its dependencies on other actors and systems.
- The decisions, actions, and outcomes required to perform its responsibilities.
- Additional capabilities reasonably implied by domain and niche knowledge.

Do not restrict discovery to the actions, permissions, fields, or capabilities
explicitly mentioned in the generated content.

Ask:

"What should this actor reasonably be able to do in this business, even if
the supplied artifact does not mention it?"

"Given this actor's purpose and responsibilities, what other meaningful
capabilities are necessary or reasonably expected?"

"What can this actor not currently accomplish because a capability,
permission, interaction, or operational step is missing?"

"What capabilities would be relevant to this actor in this operating model,
but are not justified or applicable to other operating models?"

Derive the answers independently for every actor.

Do not use a predefined catalogue of actors, capabilities, features, actions,
business objects, or lifecycle stages as the definition of completeness.

### 2.2 Explore the actor's complete operational responsibilities

For each actor, reason from its purpose to the work it must perform.

Determine:

- What outcomes the actor is responsible for achieving.
- What information and business entities it needs to interact with.
- What it needs to initiate, create, perform, maintain, or manage.
- What it needs to inspect, decide, authorize, execute, or complete.
- What changes it may need to make and which changes it must not make.
- What conditions must be satisfied before it can act.
- What it needs from other actors and systems.
- What happens after it performs an action.
- How it knows whether the expected outcome was achieved.
- What it must do when the expected outcome cannot be achieved.
- What additional capabilities are implied by the above but absent from
  the supplied content.

These are reasoning questions, not mandatory feature categories. Follow the
business context wherever it leads.

Do not stop when the actor's declared responsibilities appear to be covered.
Independently examine whether the responsibilities themselves are sufficient
and whether other material capabilities are missing.

Do not assume that every possible capability must be added. Determine whether
it is relevant to the business, role, and operating model.

### 2.3 Discover capabilities beyond the existing data

The supplied content may describe only a subset of what an actor can do.

Actively search for additional, contextually justified capabilities by
reasoning from the actor's role, the business outcomes, and the operating model.

Do not require the missing capability to have an existing field, permission,
action name, business object, or workflow step in the supplied artifact.

The absence of an existing representation is not evidence that the capability
is irrelevant.

Conversely, the fact that a capability is common in other businesses does
not establish that it is required here.

For every candidate capability, independently establish:

1. Why the actor would need it in this business.
2. Which responsibility, outcome, interaction, or operating-model expectation
   it supports.
3. Whether it is applicable to the inferred business model.
4. Whether it is already represented under another name or implemented
   through an existing actor, system, or process.
5. What specifically is missing if the capability is not adequately covered.
6. What operational consequence follows from the omission.
7. What minimum change would resolve the deficiency.

Only report a capability as a confirmed gap when a meaningful omission can
be established. If applicability or policy remains unresolved, distinguish
that uncertainty from a confirmed defect.

### 2.4 Examine the implications of each capability

For every material capability candidate, reason about its necessary
operational implications.

Determine whether the actor needs relevant information, permission,
preconditions, validation, execution, resulting state changes, downstream
effects, feedback, and exception handling.

Identify missing implications only when they are necessary or reasonably
expected in the business context.

Do not automatically turn every capability into a separate workflow,
permission, business object, or finding.

Prefer one coherent capability correction when it resolves several related
omissions, while keeping genuinely independent problems separate.

## 3. ANALYSIS B — CROSS-ACTOR OPERATIONAL GAP DISCOVERY
============================================================

Independently inspect how the business operates across actors and systems.

Do not limit this analysis to the responsibilities or capabilities of any
single actor.

### 3.1 Responsibility and ownership continuity

Determine whether material business responsibilities have clear ownership.

Identify cases where:
- A required activity has no accountable owner.
- An actor is assigned an outcome but lacks the authority or capability to
  achieve it.
- Multiple actors have conflicting or ambiguous ownership.
- A handoff leaves the next actor unable to proceed.
- A necessary decision has no defined decision authority.
- A responsibility is assumed to be covered but is not actually executed
  by any actor or system.

Do not assume that every missing function requires a new human role.

### 3.2 Operational dependencies and handoffs

Trace how actors and systems depend on each other.

Determine whether:
- Required inputs are available before an activity starts.
- The output of one activity supports the next activity.
- Ownership and responsibility transfer correctly.
- Required decisions, acknowledgements, or results reach the relevant parties.
- A downstream activity starts only when its prerequisites are satisfied.
- Changes in one record or process leave dependent activities consistent.
- The final outcome is known to the appropriate actor or system.

Identify specific broken or incomplete dependencies, not hypothetical
improvements without a demonstrated business need.

### 3.3 Decisions, transitions, and lifecycle continuity

Examine the business process from initiation through completion.

Determine whether the supplied artifact adequately represents the meaningful
decisions, permitted transitions, preconditions, postconditions, and outcomes
required by the inferred operating model.

Identify missing or contradictory transitions, premature actions, unreachable
outcomes, inconsistent states, unhandled rejection paths, or incomplete
completion and closure behavior where applicable.

Do not impose a universal lifecycle or state model on every business object.

Derive relevant lifecycle requirements from the actual business context.

### 3.4 Exceptions and recovery across actors

Independently reason about what happens when relevant operations fail or
expected outcomes cannot be achieved.

Consider contextual failure scenarios involving invalid inputs, rejected
decisions, unavailable dependencies, duplicate execution, delays, partial
completion, cancellations, reversals, and other relevant exceptions.

Determine:
- Who detects the condition.
- Who or what is responsible for the next action.
- Whether the required information and permissions are available.
- How affected records and dependent activities remain consistent.
- Whether retry, remediation, escalation, or an alternative outcome is needed.
- How the process reaches a valid terminal or recoverable condition.

These are investigative questions. Do not report every possible failure
scenario as a separate requirement.

### 3.5 Cross-actor authorization and control

Determine whether actors have the appropriate authority for their
responsibilities and interactions.

Identify material conflicts, excessive permissions, missing permissions,
inappropriate approval boundaries, unverified ownership, or missing controls
where justified by the business context.

Distinguish between an actor requesting an operation, approving it, executing
it, and recording its outcome.

Do not invent mandatory separation of duties, approval layers, organizational
hierarchies, or control requirements without contextual justification.

recommended_change or business_clarification.

confidence:

For ADD, provide only the new fragment that belongs in the actual artifact.

For UPDATE, provide only the smallest replacement value or object fragment.

be removed.

object, review_action to business_clarification, and explain the unresolved
decision in placement_instruction.

Use NO_CHANGE only for material uncertainty that prevents a defensible
correction. Do not use it to avoid correcting a confirmed gap.

Never invent an existing identifier, field, permission, actor, JSON path,
or artifact structure.

If the exact path cannot be verified, set target_path to null and identify
the closest verifiable location in placement_instruction.

Every finding must reference exactly one problem_id from the problems array.
Every problem must have exactly one corresponding finding.

Do not include undeclared fields in either array.

## 9. VALIDATE THE COMPLETE REVIEW
============================================================

Before returning the result, verify that:

1. Both independent analyses were performed: actor capability discovery
   and cross-actor operational gap discovery.
2. Existing actors were examined for capabilities beyond those explicitly
   supplied in the artifact.
3. Capabilities were derived from the actual domain, niche, actor purpose,
   and inferred operating model rather than a fixed business-specific list.
4. Cross-actor ownership, handoffs, dependencies, authorization boundaries,
   and operational outcomes were examined.
5. Each reported gap describes a real, contextually justified omission,
   contradiction, or material uncertainty.
6. Optional capabilities are not presented as confirmed requirements.
7. Existing actors and systems were considered before introducing new actors.
8. Each correction addresses its underlying problem with the smallest
   justified change.
9. Target paths and artifact structures are verified or explicitly null.
10. Proposed content is valid JSON and compatible with the actual target.
11. No correction introduces unsupported requirements, conflicting
    responsibilities, or excessive permissions.
12. Every problem has exactly one matching finding.
13. Every problem_id is unique and every finding references an existing ID.
14. Character limits and the exact output schema are respected.
15. The original artifact has not been regenerated or modified.

If no material gaps or material clarification requirements are identified,
return an adequate assessment with empty problems and findings.

Do not create findings merely because additional capabilities are conceivable.

============================================================
## OUTPUT CONTRACT
============================================================

Return valid JSON only.

Do not return Markdown fences, explanations, commentary, or additional
top-level properties.

Use exactly this structure:

{
  "overall_assessment": "adequate | gaps_identified | clarification_required",
  "problems": [
    {
      "problem_id": "P001",
      "problem": "Specific gap or inconsistency, maximum 160 characters.",
      "business_rationale": "Why it matters, maximum 200 characters.",
      "proposed_flow": "Recommended actor/action sequence or capability, maximum 160 characters."
    }
  ],
  "findings": [
    {
      "problem_id": "P001",
      "target_path": "Exact verified JSON path or null",
      "correction_type": "ADD | UPDATE | REMOVE | NO_CHANGE",
      "proposed_content": {},
      "placement_instruction": "Precise correction or clarification instructions.",
      "review_action": "recommended_change | business_clarification",
      "confidence": "high | medium | low"
    }
  ]
}

### FINAL OUTPUT RULES

- Populate problems with every distinct, meaningful, validated gap.
- Populate findings with exactly one corresponding correction per problem.
- Keep each problem independently reviewable.
- Do not omit a material problem merely because its correction is uncertain.
- For material uncertainty, use NO_CHANGE and business_clarification without
  inventing corrective content.
- For ADD, provide only the new JSON fragment.
- For UPDATE, provide only the minimal replacement value or object fragment.
- For REMOVE, identify the exact element to remove.
- For NO_CHANGE, use an empty proposed_content object.
- Use null for an unverified target_path.
- Do not add undeclared fields or additional top-level properties.
- Do not regenerate or modify the original artifact.
- Use "gaps_identified" when actionable material gaps exist.
- Use "clarification_required" when a material business decision is unresolved.
  This may coexist with actionable gaps.
- Use "adequate" with empty arrays when neither material gaps nor material
  clarification requirements remain.
