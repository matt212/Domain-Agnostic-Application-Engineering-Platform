### 3. CORE DISCOVERY OBJECTIVE

For every Parent Information Requirement, independently discover and elaborate the following five dimensions:

1. information_requirement
2. information_usage
3. information_source
4. business_decisions_supported
5. required_information_details

The Parent Information Requirement is the anchor for the entire analysis.

Do not create new Parent Information Requirements, rename existing ones, combine them, split them, or omit them.

Process each requirement independently and completely.

---

### 4. QUESTION-DRIVEN DISCOVERY METHODOLOGY

Before generating the output for each Parent Information Requirement, conduct an internal investigation by asking and answering the questions below.

These questions are analytical prompts, not mandatory output fields. Use the questions that are relevant to the specific requirement, and introduce additional domain-specific questions wherever necessary.

#### 4.1 Investigate Information Usage

First establish why the business needs this information and how it is actually used.

Ask:

- What business problem does this information solve?
- Why does the business need this information?
- What would happen if this information were unavailable, inaccurate, incomplete, or outdated?
- Which business processes or operational activities depend on it?
- At what point in a business process is the information required?
- Which actors need to consume, access, interpret, or act upon it?
- What responsibility does each actor perform using this information?
- Is the information needed before an activity, during execution, or after completion?
- Is it required continuously, periodically, on demand, or when a business event occurs?
- Does the information support operational execution, monitoring, control, compliance, financial management, customer service, or strategic decisions?
- Does one activity produce information that another activity subsequently consumes?
- Does the information need to be shared between actors, business functions, or external parties?
- What operational exceptions or business scenarios make this information particularly important?
- What business consequences arise when the information is incorrect or unavailable?

Use the answers to explain the actual business purpose, consumers, operational dependencies, timing, and outcomes.

Do not produce generic descriptions such as "used to support business operations." Explain precisely how and why the information is used in the identified domain and niche.

#### 4.2 Investigate Information Sources

Next establish where the information originates and how the business obtains it.

Ask:

- Who or what originally generates this information?
- Which business actor is responsible for creating, maintaining, confirming, or updating it?
- Which business object contains or represents the information?
- Which business activity generates or changes the information?
- Is the information internally generated, externally provided, derived, calculated, observed, or collected?
- Does it originate from a transaction, operational event, business record, external service, regulatory source, physical activity, or another information source?
- Does the business depend on another organization, partner, service provider, or external authority to obtain it?
- What logical system or source category would normally provide this information in this domain?
- How is the information acquired: direct entry, transaction capture, system integration, event notification, periodic synchronization, measurement, or another mechanism?
- Does the information require verification, reconciliation, enrichment, or transformation before it can be trusted?
- How frequently must the source provide updates?
- What event would trigger an update?
- What happens when the source is unavailable, delayed, inconsistent, or unreliable?
- Is there a secondary or fallback source?
- How can the business establish the origin, freshness, and reliability of the information?

Identify the logical source categories and acquisition mechanisms appropriate to the domain.

You may infer relevant categories such as operational systems, partner systems, financial services, external data providers, regulatory sources, physical records, or business-generated information where justified.

However:

- Do not claim that a specific vendor, product, application, API, or integration is deployed unless the input explicitly confirms it.
- Distinguish logical source requirements from confirmed existing integrations.
- Do not invent named systems or organizations.
- Explain the information's origin and acquisition mechanism, not merely the name of a business object.

#### 4.3 Investigate Business Decisions Supported

Determine what actual business decisions depend on the information.

Ask:

- What decision becomes possible because this information is available?
- Who makes the decision: a business actor, an authorized role, or an automated business function?
- At which business activity or decision point is it made?
- What information is evaluated before the decision?
- What conditions, alternatives, or business circumstances influence the decision?
- What possible outcomes can result?
- Does the decision authorize, reject, approve, select, allocate, prioritize, assign, release, suspend, escalate, reconcile, or trigger another action?
- Does the decision affect a customer, transaction, business object, operational resource, financial position, or compliance obligation?
- Does the decision need to be revisited when information changes?
- What decisions are required when information is missing, conflicting, invalid, or outside acceptable business conditions?
- What decisions require human intervention rather than automated processing?
- What downstream business activities depend on the decision outcome?

Identify specific business decisions rather than merely listing activities.

For each decision, explain the actual choice being made and the business purpose it serves.

Examples of decision types to consider, only where relevant:

- Eligibility and qualification.
- Approval and authorization.
- Selection and prioritization.
- Allocation and assignment.
- Pricing and financial calculation.
- Risk assessment and exception handling.
- Operational scheduling and resource planning.
- Compliance and policy enforcement.
- Reconciliation and settlement.
- Escalation, suspension, cancellation, or recovery.

Do not force every decision category into every requirement. Discover decisions from the actual domain context.

#### 4.4 Investigate Required Information Details

Determine the specific information elements the business needs to fulfil the requirement.

Ask:

- What exact information must be known to satisfy this requirement?
- What attributes, facts, measurements, classifications, identifiers, and statuses are necessary?
- What distinguishes one relevant business entity, transaction, event, location, resource, or record from another?
- What quantities, values, dates, times, durations, or measurements are needed?
- What conditions or classifications affect how the information is interpreted?
- What lifecycle status or progress information is required?
- What information identifies ownership, responsibility, authorization, or accountability?
- What contextual information is needed to understand the circumstances in which the information was created?
