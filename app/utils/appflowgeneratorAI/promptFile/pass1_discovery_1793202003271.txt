2. REQUIRED DISCOVERY APPROACH
==================================================

Perform the analysis in four stages.

STAGE A — EVIDENCE EXTRACTION

For every actor, identify all distinct information domains evidenced
by the supplied data.

Examine the information the actor can access, create, change, or
operate on, along with the associated business objects, information
entities, activities, and interactions.

Do not limit the analysis to the actor's primary journey or most
prominent activity.

Do not assume that the first apparent system represents the actor's
entire functional scope.

STAGE B — BUSINESS INFORMATION SYSTEM DISCOVERY

Independently classify the extracted evidence into distinct
functional business information systems.

A business information system is a coherent functional domain
concerned with managing, accessing, processing, or operating
business information.

Discover system boundaries from the evidence and functional purpose.

Keep distinct information domains separate when they represent
materially different business purposes. Group related evidence
when it serves substantially the same functional purpose.

Do not create one system for every individual object, action,
screen, or field. Equally, do not combine multiple distinct systems
into one umbrella system merely because they participate in the
same business journey or transaction.

Discover the appropriate system names from the actual evidence.
Do not use a predefined list of system names, actor-to-system
mappings, industry templates, fixed system counts, or default
systems.

Do not assume that an actor has only one business information system.

Do not assume that a system is separately deployed software.
Classify functional systems, not deployment architecture.

A system may be relevant to multiple actors, but assess each
actor-system relationship independently.

STAGE C — INDUSTRY TERMINOLOGY AND EVIDENCE

For each discovered system, identify:

1. Business information system:
   A concise, accurate name for the functional system.

2. Common industry terminology:
   Established industry terms that accurately describe that
   system's functional scope.

3. Evidence in the supplied data:
   The actual source information, objects, capabilities, activities,
   or interactions supporting the classification.

Evidence must be traceable to the supplied input. Do not invent
source fields, objects, permissions, or activities.

Distinguish direct evidence from reasonable inference internally.
A system may be inferred when the supplied evidence supports a
distinct functional purpose, even if the system is not explicitly
named.

Industry terminology must describe the identified system rather
than merely list related keywords, technologies, screens, actors,
or business objects.

Do not combine unrelated system categories into one system name
or present overlapping terms as synonyms without justification.

STAGE D — NEW MANDATORY CAPABILITY DISCOVERY

For every actor-system relationship, independently identify any
new mandatory capabilities justified by the evidence.

Compare proposed capabilities semantically against the actor's
existing capability and authorization data.

Return only genuinely new capabilities that are not already covered
by existing entries.

Respect the actor's existing authorization scope. Do not invent
permissions or grant broader access.

==================================================
3. COMPLETENESS: DO NOT MISS DISTINCT SYSTEMS
==================================================

Completeness is mandatory.

After discovering the initial systems for an actor, revisit all
available evidence for that actor.

Identify information domains that may have been overlooked because
they are secondary to the actor's main journey or closely related
to another system.

Check whether distinct functional purposes have been incorrectly
merged into a broad system.

Check whether evidence concerning different information domains
has been absorbed into a single system without a functional reason.

Check whether any system classification is too broad to represent
the distinct purposes evidenced by the input.

Where the evidence supports multiple distinct systems, return
multiple actor-system objects.

Do not force every actor into a fixed number of systems.

Do not add systems merely to achieve completeness by count.
Completeness means covering all distinct, justified functional
domains evidenced in the supplied data.

Do not omit a justified system merely because its name does not
appear verbatim in the source.

Do not merge distinct systems merely because they exchange data,
support the same transaction, or appear in the same actor journey.

Do not split a single coherent system into artificial systems
without evidence of a meaningful functional distinction.

==================================================
4. SYSTEM DISCOVERY MUST BE EVIDENCE-DRIVEN
==================================================

Use the supplied domain and niche to interpret business context.

Use the actor data and associated information to establish the
actor's involvement and authority.

Industry knowledge may help identify the appropriate terminology
or interpret a functional domain, but it cannot independently
establish that the actor has a particular capability.

Do not invent business processes, information entities, system
integrations, regulatory obligations, permissions, approval
authority, organizational structures, or operational rules.

Do not assume that every actor who uses information can also
create, modify, approve, or execute operations involving it.

Do not use generic enterprise architecture conventions as a
substitute for source-based classification.

Do not hardcode system names, expected actors, example systems,
capability lists, or actor-to-system mappings into the analysis.

The discovery process must remain applicable to different domains,
niches, and business models.

5. CAPABILITY ARRAY DEFINITIONS
==================================================

For every discovered actor-system relationship, populate these
four arrays with only genuinely new, mandatory capabilities.

What_They_Can_See:
New mandatory information-access capabilities within the system.

What_They_Can_Create:
New mandatory capabilities for creating relevant information,
records, requests, or transactions within the system.

What_They_Can_Change:
New mandatory capabilities for modifying existing information
or records within the system.

Operations_They_Can_Run:
New mandatory capabilities for executing relevant operational
actions within the system.

Apply these rules:

- Discover additions from the actual input evidence.
- Do not copy existing capabilities into the output.
- Do not paraphrase existing entries and present them as new.
- Do not duplicate capabilities already covered semantically.
- Do not infer permission solely from an actor's involvement
  in a business journey.
- Do not expand the actor's existing authority without evidence.
- Do not populate an array simply because the property exists.
- Multiple additions are allowed when independently justified.
- Empty arrays are valid when no new capability is justified.

If an actor already has all the required capabilities for a system,
return empty arrays for the relevant categories.

The four arrays describe incremental additions, not the actor's
complete permission set.
