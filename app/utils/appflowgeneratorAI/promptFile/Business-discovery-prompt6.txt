TASK

Using ONLY the supplied INTERNET SEARCH RESULTS:

1. Determine the closest real-world PRIMARY REFERENCE BUSINESS.
2. Determine the primary BUSINESS DOMAIN.
3. Identify evidence-supported BUSINESS OBJECTS.
4. Identify evidence-supported BUSINESS ACTIVITIES.
5. Map each Business Object to the Activities that materially handle it.
6. Identify relevant evidence-supported lifecycle handling.
7. Identify only materially different evidence-supported alternative paths.

RULES

- Internet Search Results are the only factual evidence.
- Do not use general industry assumptions.
- Do not invent capabilities.
- Do not infer that a capability exists because similar businesses have it.
- Do not include UI elements, screens, pages, buttons, APIs, databases,
  technical components, attributes, rules, or decisions as Business Objects.
- Do not create an Activity merely for validation, calculation,
  authorization, notification, status change, or UI interaction.
- Do not split one real-world business operation into artificial Activities.
- Do not create Alternative Paths for ordinary UI choices or field values.
- Every Business Object MUST be handled by at least one Activity.
- Every Activity MUST materially handle at least one Business Object.
- Every Activity Step MUST relate to the Activity's Business Objects.
- Every Alternative Path MUST materially change the business process.
- Prefer a smaller evidence-supported model over an inferred model.
- Do not add anything that cannot be supported by the supplied evidence.



IMPORTANT DISCOVERY REQUIREMENTS

1. PRIMARY REFERENCE BUSINESS

The PRIMARY REFERENCE BUSINESS must be a specific real-world
company or business supported by the supplied Internet evidence.

Do NOT return a generic business type.

WRONG:
"online retail store"
"e-commerce company"
"grocery business"

The result must identify a specific established business when the
Internet evidence supports one.

2. BUSINESS DOMAIN

The BUSINESS DOMAIN must describe the actual operating model rather
than a broad industry category.

WRONG:
"e-commerce"

BETTER:
"online grocery retail"
"online grocery marketplace"
"grocery delivery marketplace"

Choose the most specific domain supported by the evidence.

6. ACTIVITY MATERIALITY

Do NOT create separate Business Activities for:

notifications
confirmations
validation
authorization
calculations
UI actions
buttons
screens
status changes
simple decisions

When these occur as part of a larger business operation, represent
them as steps, rules, decisions, or alternative paths as appropriate.


8. ACTIVITY CONSOLIDATION

Before creating a Business Activity, determine whether the operation
is materially distinct from an existing Activity.

Do NOT create a separate Activity when the operation is merely:

- a step within another Activity
- an alternative path within another Activity
- a validation within another Activity
- a payment/refund action within a larger transaction lifecycle
- a notification or confirmation
- a simple substitution or exception within fulfillment
- a status transition

Create a separate Business Activity only when it represents a
materially distinct business operation with its own business purpose,
lifecycle handling, and business objects.

Prefer:

Order Fulfillment
  → normal fulfillment
  → item unavailable
  → substitution
  → cancellation

over creating separate Activities for every exception.

Prefer a coherent end-to-end business lifecycle over artificial
Activity fragmentation.

7. EVIDENCE-FIRST BUT NOT MINIMAL

The objective is:

COMPLETE EVIDENCE-SUPPORTED BUSINESS MODEL

NOT:

MINIMUM POSSIBLE BUSINESS MODEL.

Do not invent missing capabilities.

However, do not unnecessarily remove important Business Objects,
Business Activities, lifecycle handling, or Alternative Paths when
the supplied Internet evidence supports them.

Prefer the complete evidence-supported model over an artificially
small model.
